Five Stunning Places 03  /  Banff, Canada
No. 03

Banff, where lakes forgot how to be blue.

Canada  ·  Alberta  ·  51.18°N, 115.55°W

Moraine Lake at first light, held in the Valley of the Ten Peaks.

01
The Canadian Rockies

A national park the size of a small country

Banff National Park is Canada's oldest, 6,600 square kilometers of Rocky Mountain wilderness in the western province of Alberta. It is the kind of place that does not pose for photos so much as make them feel a bit inadequate — the peaks are too close, the air is too clear, the scale is wrong. Three hours west of Calgary, you drive through ordinary prairie and then, with no warning, the mountains start.

02
The turquoise lakes

Lake Louise and Moraine Lake

The two most famous lakes — Louise and Moraine — are both fed by glaciers, and the meltwater carries a fine stone dust called rock flour that scatters light in a very specific way. The result is a color that actually hurts your eyes a little: not green, not blue, something between a swimming pool and a bottle of mouthwash. It looks fake in photos. It looks fake in real life, too. You just stand there and accept it.

03
Wildlife

The park that has more bears than people

Banff is home to grizzlies, black bears, wolves, moose, elk, bighorn sheep, mountain goats, cougars, and a surprisingly assertive population of chipmunks. They are not behind fences; they are walking along the roads. Drivers routinely stop for elk crossings at rush hour. The town has wildlife corridors built under the highways. It is the only place I know where the warning signs are genuinely sincere.

04
The town itself

Hot springs, quiet streets, and a castle

The town of Banff sits inside the park, wrapped by mountains on every side. There is one main street, lined with coffee shops, outdoor stores, and maple-everything bakeries. Just above the town, the hot springs have been running at the same temperature since before the park existed, and the Fairmont Banff Springs Hotel looks like a Scottish castle was set down, by accident, in the middle of Alberta.

05
Why mountains

Why mountains, in general, help

Whatever is bothering you at sea level, mountains have already outlasted a version of it. A ten-minute walk from any trailhead in Banff puts you in a valley where nothing has changed in ten thousand years, and there is something deeply steadying about that. You do not conquer the Rockies. You just borrow a few days and they let you come back feeling slightly reassembled.

“Climb the mountains and get their good tidings. Nature's peace will flow into you as sunshine flows into trees.” John Muir, 1901
