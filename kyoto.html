Five Stunning Places 01  /  Kyoto, Japan
No. 01

Kyoto, a city of a thousand gates.

Japan  ·  Kansai  ·  35.01°N, 135.77°E

The senbon torii path at Fushimi Inari, a tunnel of ten thousand gates.

01
The old capital

A thousand-year hush

Kyoto was Japan's imperial capital for more than a thousand years, and it still walks like a city that knows this. Tokyo hums; Kyoto breathes. Narrow wooden houses called machiya lean into quiet side streets, temple bells carry across the rooftops at dusk, and even the shinkansen arriving from Tokyo feels like it has agreed, somehow, to soften its voice the moment it crosses into the valley.

02
Fushimi Inari

Ten thousand torii

The shrine of Fushimi Inari climbs a small forested mountain south of the city, and the whole climb is lined with vermilion torii gates — one donated for every business blessing, every wish granted, every quiet promise made. Arrive at sunrise, before the tour groups, and you can walk for an hour under a tunnel of red light that flickers like a paper lantern you are somehow walking inside.

03
The golden temple

Kinkaku-ji and its mirror

Kinkaku-ji — the Golden Pavilion — is a small three-story temple wrapped, floor to floor, in real gold leaf. It sits on a pond so still that on windless mornings the temple appears twice, once in the air and once in the water, and it is genuinely hard to tell which copy is the real one. The original was burned to the ground in 1950 by a novice monk who could not bear how beautiful it was; the current version, rebuilt faithfully, carries that story quietly.

04
Seasons

Cherry blossoms, then maple fire

Twice a year Kyoto becomes a stage. In early April the cherry trees along the Philosopher's Path open for about ten days and the entire city tilts into a pale-pink fever dream. In late November the maples turn deep red and gold and everyone lights up the temple gardens at night, and it stops being a city and becomes a painting that has forgotten to dry. The rest of the year, Kyoto is beautiful. Those two weeks, it is almost unfair.

05
Night & food

Small rooms, small meals

Kyoto's great pleasure is miniature. Eight-seat sushi counters in Pontocho. Seven-course kaiseki dinners where each bowl is the size of your palm. A matcha served on a bench overlooking a gravel garden that took someone forty years to rake right. It is a city that quietly insists beauty is a craft, not an accident, and that paying attention is itself a form of respect.

“In Kyoto, even the silence seems to be arranged on purpose.” A note I made, walking home after dinner
